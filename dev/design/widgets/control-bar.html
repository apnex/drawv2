The top control bar — on the diagram-as-UI convention

The real #menu bar (app/index.html) rendered as a diagram, three ways. Its 11 controls fall in 3 clusters: file (logo · name · open ▾ · + · ×) · slides (url · ⇑ slides) · status (? · online). The question: one panel, or a zone of three panels?

The model gives two composition primitives: a panel is ONE entity whose parts are content regions (not separately addressable as graph nodes); a zone is a container that groups several entities (panels/nodes). So the choice is semantic — are the clusters regions of one widget, or separate widgets? Both respect "no recursive nesting": A has one hull; in B/C the zone wraps sibling panels (it is not a hull-in-hull). Buttons/inputs stay clickable either way (clickable is an orthogonal behaviour on a region, not a reason to split panels).
Chosen — A (single panel). A control bar is one cohesive strip, not a composition of independent widgets; expressing it as one panel matches the real <header> and our settled "a form / control-bar is ONE panel" rule, and keeps it the lightest. The online indicator stays a pill — a content region with a socket border + solid green fill + dark text + pill radius (the .lock-unlocked style). Reserve B (zone + panels) for when the clusters are genuinely separate, reusable widgets you compose on a canvas (a dashboard), and C when you want them addressable without the zone backdrop. The clusters in A can still be set off with a thin separator content region if more visual division is wanted.
