# H26.6 -- pan and zoom: an infinite canvas, one grid per diagram, each viewer's own view

The design of record for H26.6, from the director's intent captured in `PAN-ZOOM-INTENT.md` and ruled as `H26-22`.\
Draw's canvas loses its edges: one grid per diagram, 60 by default and changeable, devices at its square centres and zones on its lines; a viewer pans and zooms with the touchpad and mouse gestures the intent names, and each viewer's view is remembered per diagram in their browser, with a reticle button home to 0,0.\
It overturns decision 1's fixed canvas and WD7's refusal of other grid values once ratified, and meets B289 and B325; it is built component first, as H26-3 ordered and H26-22 confirms, on the four components draw runs on -- `input-binding`, `input-capture` and `vector-scene` `0.2.0`, `snap-grid` `0.1.0` -- and the gesture tester.

**Standing:** revision 6, not ratified: the director's walkthrough has ruled PZ6, PZ7, PZ12 and PZ27 and clarified Q3 -- devices keep their square -- on which the size change is rebuilt by lattice index, PZ1, PZ2 and PZ3 closed by it, PZ19's recommendation turned and PZ21 re-measured to be put again (section 9); reading 1's 32 findings disposed of in section 8.1, reading 2's 26 in 8.2, reading 3's 2 in 8.3, reading 4's 3 in 8.4 and reading 5's 2 in 8.5, carried into appendix A, reading 5 having found reading 4's three folds holding; awaiting one confirming reading scoped to revision 6's design-level changes (8.5); the code read is `1fbbc5b`, and no product file has changed since (measured again at `a8a32c7`); code lines measured by `tools/code-split.mjs` and its `codeLines`, browser behaviour in headless Chrome through `@apnex/headless-chromium` on draw's lab page, the scripts and their outputs at `~/taceng/drawv2-eval-runs/h26-pan-zoom/probes/`.

It does not decide:
- anything the intent's anti-goals exclude: a view stored in the diagram or shared between viewers (AG-1), the wheel zooming without Ctrl or a drag on open ground panning (AG-2), one grid size for every diagram or the two grids sized apart (AG-3), a device scaling with its grid (AG-4), or draw built before the components it needs (AG-5);
- a touch screen's or a pen's gestures, which the intent does not name: their pointers keep today's meaning;
- the stored document's form beyond its grid, or draw's configuration as data (B425);
- any behaviour change beyond those section 5 names, each ruled with this design.

### 2.3 The grid's size is also a device's unit

`STD.pitch` (`kernel/spec.mjs:10`) is derived from a device's frame -- a 40 frame, six ladder steps of 3 and a gutter of 2 make 60 (`kernel/spec.mjs:5`) -- and is read as the grid's spacing by snapping, the clamp, the nudge and the readout through `GAP`.\
It is read as a device's own unit too: a device's sockets a pitch apart in its own frame, its content regions' cells, and its span's footprint, found by `span` on the device grid (`plugins/devices/device-fields.mjs:72`, `plugins/devices/device-footprint.mjs:20`, `plugins/devices/device-gestures.mjs:27`, `server/svg.mjs:101`).\
It is the simulation's unit of speed and range, which it names cells, chosen "so a stored speed keeps its meaning if the pitch ever changes" (`plugins/simulation/movers.mjs:42-47`, B172), and the occupancy index keys each device by its cell on the device grid.\
So the pitch is named on 29 lines in 15 files and `NODE_GRID`'s `span` and `nearest` asked on 11 lines in 8, and the intent's Q3 -- a size change keeps every device's own size -- needs each of them read either as the diagram's grid or as a device's unit that stays 60, each classified in 3.9.\

### 3.2 What each component's contract gains

**`vector-scene` `0.3.0` -- a view** (PZ22; B419; its D6 as the director refined it; revised after reading 1, o:F1, o:F2, x:F1, and reading 2, o:F1, a:F1, a:F2):
- **asked beside a root**: `view: true` is a member of the declaration `openScene` judges, refused with the problem `view-without-root` when no root is given, so the canvas's declaration, which the export opens with no root (`server/svg.mjs:122`), stays viewless and its bytes unchanged, and the renderer adds the view where it adds the root (`app/src/renderer.js:78`); a declaration without it is exactly `0.2.0`'s; a viewed declaration whose layer declares a `transform` attribute is refused (`layer-transform`), that attribute being the view's;
  read as VS2 rule 2 reads a declaration, the descriptors of `root`, `layers` and `view` in that order, `view` a data property holding a boolean, `false` the same as absent; VS3's table gains its rows -- step 2 admits `view` beside `root` and `layers`; after step 4, `view` an accessor or not a boolean is `invalid-value` at `/view`, and `view` `true` with no `root` is `view-without-root` at `/view`, a `root` present but refused being `/root`'s problem alone; after step 7, with `view` `true`, a layer whose valid `attrs` holds `transform` is `layer-transform` at `/layers/<i>/attrs/transform`, nothing of it reported while `view` is refused or `false`;
- **the view** is `{ x, y, scale }`: the drawing point at the root's top-left corner, and the root's own units per drawing unit, which with no `viewBox` are CSS pixels; it opens at `{ x: 0, y: 0, scale: 1 }`, written on each layer's element as the layer is made, so no frame shows a layer unviewed;
- **`setView(view)`** reads its argument as VS2 rule 1 reads a value, a plain object of exactly `x`, `y` and `scale`, own data properties, each a finite number and `scale` above 0, and throws a `TypeError` beginning `setView` at the first that is not, reading nothing after it and leaving the view as it was; then the scene's state, an `Error` if it is closed, failed, writing or declared without a view (VS3's lifecycle rule 4); then it computes the transform's members, `s`, `-s * x` and `-s * y`, each once in binary64, and throws a `TypeError` beginning `setView` if any is not finite -- `{ x: 1e308, y: 0, scale: 2 }` gives `-Infinity` -- or if the view lies outside the range the scene supports -- `scale` from 2^-16 to 2^16, and each member's magnitude at most 2^24 -- before any platform write, the view as it was (restated after reading 3, o:F2, and reading 4, o:F2);
  the platform holds a transform in single precision, where binary64's finite numbers are not all representable -- `Math.fround(1e39)` is `Infinity`, `Math.fround(1e-50)` is 0 -- so the range is set where single precision still holds what is written, and measured at its edges in headless Chrome: at scales 2^-16 and 2^16 a bar is drawn within 0.004 pixels and hit within 0.125 of where 3.2's arithmetic puts it, at 2^-20 it is drawn nowhere; at a member of 2^24 it is drawn 1 pixel out and hit 2 out, single precision's own resolution there; at a scale of 1e39 or 1e-50, or a member of 1e39, nothing is drawn (`transform-range.mjs`);
  the range bounds what the scene writes, not where an application puts its shapes: a shape a trillion units out is drawn nowhere even inside it, so the precision of what is drawn stays the application's to hold -- draw's by PZ31's bound and PZ12's levels, whose scales lie between 0.165 and 1.57 on the windows measured (`zoom-levels.mjs`), which keep every member below 2 million and every coordinate within 2^20; otherwise it writes `transform="matrix(s, 0, 0, s, -s * x, -s * y)"`, each member written by `String`, on every layer's element, scene and foreign alike, the elements it alone writes on (VS4), and answers the view written; it holds the scene's writing state while it writes, so a getter or a trap the platform runs cannot re-enter it; a platform write that throws leaves the scene failed, as any write does (VS7); the transform depends on the view alone -- no measurement enters it -- so neither the root's size on screen nor a resize can make it wrong (o:F1, o:F2); `view()` answers the view written;
- **`visible()`** answers `{ region, scale }` in `snap-grid`'s view form (SG6), handed to `inView` unchanged: `region` is `{ kind: 'rect', x, y, w: W / s, h: H / s }` -- `kind` among its members, which `inView` requires and revision 2 left out (reading 2, o:F1) -- `W` and `H` the root's `clientWidth` and then `clientHeight`, read at the call -- its size before any CSS transform, in its own units -- and `scale` the screen pixels per drawing unit, `s` times the root's screen scale, `Math.hypot(a, b)` of its `getScreenCTM`; a closed rect, which `inView` reads as SG6 says; exact for a root whose screen transform is a translation and a uniform scale, as draw's is; a root with padding, or rotated or skewed, is outside what `visible()` promises, the conversions staying exact for any; it reads `clientWidth`, `clientHeight` and then the screen transform as VS10 reads one, and throws an `Error` for a scene closed, failed or declared without a view, a width or height that is not a finite number at least 0, a transform VS10 refuses, or an answer whose members are not finite, whose far edges, `x + w` and `y + h` computed in binary64, are not finite -- `snap-grid` refuses a region whose far edge is not -- or whose `scale` is not above 0 (restated after reading 4, o:F3), an error the platform throws propagating with the scene left open; a root not yet laid out answers a region of no width;
