# H26.6 -- pan and zoom: an infinite canvas, one grid per diagram, each viewer's own view

The design of record for H26.6, from the director's intent captured in `PAN-ZOOM-INTENT.md` and ruled as `H26-22`.\
Draw's canvas loses its edges: one grid per diagram, 60 by default and changeable, devices at its square centres and zones on its lines; a viewer pans and zooms with the touchpad and mouse gestures the intent names, and each viewer's view is remembered per diagram in their browser, with a reticle button home to 0,0.\
It overturns decision 1's fixed canvas and WD7's refusal of other grid values once ratified, and meets B289 and B325; it is built component first, as H26-3 ordered and H26-22 confirms, on the four components draw runs on -- `input-binding`, `input-capture` and `vector-scene` `0.2.0`, `snap-grid` `0.1.0` -- and the gesture tester.

**Standing:** revision 2, part A of 2, not ratified: reading 1's sixteen findings on the view interface, the size change and the pitch split folded and each disposed of in section 8.1, its other sixteen to come in part B, then a reading scoped to the design-level folds and the director's walkthrough; the code read is `1fbbc5b`, and no product file has changed since (measured again at `a8a32c7`); code lines measured by `tools/code-split.mjs` and its `codeLines`, browser behaviour in headless Chrome through `@apnex/headless-chromium` on draw's lab page, the scripts and their outputs at `~/taceng/drawv2-eval-runs/h26-pan-zoom/probes/`.

## 1. What this decides, and what it does not

It decides:
- what in draw assumes the canvas has edges or a fixed size, with its code lines, and what the four components offer and lack for a view and an unbounded grid (section 2);
- the target: what each component's contract gains, the tester's step, and draw's infinite canvas, gestures, remembered view, home button, one stored grid per diagram and the new forms of what an extent bounded today (section 3);
- the decisions owed, `PZ1` onward: each of the intent's fifteen flags and each decision the drafting finds, with options, costs, a recommendation and the evidence (section 4);
- the moves, in H26-3's order -- the components' releases, the tester on the director's Chromebook, then draw, per-diagram grid size its own step -- each with its guard and its validation in the live lab (section 5);
- the projection by part against H26.8's close (section 6), the axiom alignment audit (section 7), and the build checklist each move's builder satisfies and its verifier checks (appendix A).

It does not decide:
- anything the intent's anti-goals exclude: a view stored in the diagram or shared between viewers (AG-1), the wheel zooming without Ctrl or a drag on open ground panning (AG-2), one grid size for every diagram or the two grids sized apart (AG-3), a device scaling with its grid (AG-4), or draw built before the components it needs (AG-5);
- a touch screen's or a pen's gestures, which the intent does not name: their pointers keep today's meaning;
- the stored document's form beyond its grid, or draw's configuration as data (B425);
- any behaviour change beyond those section 5 names, each ruled with this design.

### 2.3 The grid's size is also a device's unit

`STD.pitch` (`kernel/spec.mjs:10`) is derived from a device's frame -- a 40 frame, six ladder steps of 3 and a gutter of 2 make 60 (`kernel/spec.mjs:5`) -- and is read as the grid's spacing by snapping, the clamp, the nudge and the readout through `GAP`.\
It is read as a device's own unit too: a device's sockets a pitch apart in its own frame, its content regions' cells, and its span's footprint, found by `span` on the device grid (`plugins/devices/device-fields.mjs:72`, `plugins/devices/device-footprint.mjs:20`, `plugins/devices/device-gestures.mjs:27`, `server/svg.mjs:101`).\

### 3.2 What each component's contract gains

**`vector-scene` `0.3.0` -- a view** (PZ22; B419; its D6 as the director refined it; revised after reading 1, o:F1, o:F2, x:F1):
- **asked beside a root**: `view: true` is a member of the declaration `openScene` judges, refused with the problem `view-without-root` when no root is given, so the canvas's declaration, which the export opens with no root (`server/svg.mjs:122`), stays viewless and its bytes unchanged, and the renderer adds the view where it adds the root (`app/src/renderer.js:78`); a declaration without it is exactly `0.2.0`'s; a viewed declaration whose layer declares a `transform` attribute is refused (`layer-transform`), that attribute being the view's;
- **the view** is `{ x, y, scale }`: the drawing point at the root's top-left corner, and the root's own units per drawing unit, which with no `viewBox` are CSS pixels; it opens at `{ x: 0, y: 0, scale: 1 }`, written on each layer's element as the layer is made, so no frame shows a layer unviewed;
- **`setView(view)`** refuses, the view unchanged, a member that is not a finite number or a `scale` at or below 0; otherwise it writes `transform="matrix(s, 0, 0, s, -s * x, -s * y)"`, each member evaluated once in binary64 and written by `String`, on every layer's element, scene and foreign alike, the elements it alone writes on (VS4), and answers the view written; a platform write that throws leaves the scene failed, as any write does (VS7); the transform depends on the view alone -- no measurement enters it -- so neither the root's size on screen nor a resize can make it wrong (o:F1, o:F2); `view()` answers the view written;
- **`visible()`** answers `{ region, scale }` in `snap-grid`'s view form (SG6), handed to `inView` unchanged: `region` is `{ x, y, w: W / s, h: H / s }`, `W` and `H` the root's `clientWidth` and `clientHeight` read at the call -- its size before any CSS transform, in its own units -- and `scale` the screen pixels per drawing unit, `s` times the root's screen scale, `Math.hypot(a, b)` of its `getScreenCTM`; a closed rect, which `inView` reads as SG6 says; exact for a root whose screen transform is a translation and a uniform scale, as draw's is; a root with padding, or rotated or skewed, is outside what `visible()` promises, the conversions staying exact for any;
- **`zoomAbout(screen, scale)`** answers, setting nothing, the view at `scale` that keeps the drawing point under `screen` where it is -- the root point `r` under `screen` read through the root's screen transform, the drawing point `p` under it, `x' = p.x - r.x / scale` -- so an application clamps the scale first and the point still holds;
- **`onView(listener)`** tells each layer's drawer what is visible: every listener is called, in the order registered, synchronously, with `visible()` after each `setView`; the first to throw stops the rest and its error propagates, the view written and the scene open; `onView` answers a function that removes the listener; a resize changes what the root shows, not the view, and the application, whose root's size is its own (VS13), tells the scene by setting a view again -- `setView(view())` keeps the top-left corner, draw's `view` part keeps the centre (3.7);
- **the conversions** compose the view with the root's screen transform (VS10): `toScreen(p)` is that transform applied, by VS10's arithmetic, to `(s * (p.x - x), s * (p.y - y))`, and `toDrawing(q)` is `(r.x / s + x, r.y / s + y)` for `r` the root point VS10 answers for `q`, each step in binary64 in that order, so a test computes the same numbers independently; `itemAt` and `itemOf` are unchanged, the platform's hit test seeing the layers as drawn; the root's attributes stay the application's (VS13), its `viewBox` absent for a viewed scene;
- **VS1** gains the root's `clientWidth` and `clientHeight`, read by `visible()` alone, and the `transform` attribute written on each layer element;
- **it unloads nothing**: an item out of view stays on the page, removing one being one removal already (VS6; PZ11); a layer fixed to the screen has no consumer, so none is offered.

**`snap-grid` `0.1.2` -- its build keeps its contract** (PZ13): `inView` freezes each value it makes as it makes it, tracking none, so it lists any count its contract allows where it now stops at 5,592,404 (section 2.2); a new invariant lists 2^24 positions; nothing else changes, and draw needs nothing more of it -- an unbounded grid (SG1), the nearest position a size change asks (SG3) and positions in view thinned on screen (SG6) are there today.

**`input-binding` `0.3.0` -- a threshold measured on screen** (PZ17; B404; revised after reading 1, o:F11): the session's declaration gains `measure`, `'at'` by default or `'screen'`; a declaration without it is `0.2.0`'s.\
With `'screen'` every point the session measures is a screen point, and nothing in it mixes the two:

Draw's own distance checks follow it: the link release's `atStart` (`app/src/input.js:148`) compares the release's screen point with the drag's screen start against the same 4 pixels, `chainFrom` (`app/src/input.js:1175`) hands its `from` through `toScreen`, and the palette's threshold (`app/src/palette.js:20`) is in screen pixels; no drawing-unit threshold remains in draw (a source check, appendix A move 9).

### 3.5 A size change (PZ1 to PZ4)

A person, an agent or the CLI sets the record's `pitch` as any edit (PZ29); the planner's transaction phase runs the layouts plugin's reaction, so every door gets one answer (PL-3), and the change is one commit, one undo, seen by every viewer at once (PZ4):
1. each anchor -- device or waypoint -- moves to the new device lattice's nearest point to where it stands (`nearest`, SG3, a half toward the greater; Q3: "they just move to nearest point"), a spanning device by its anchor, its first cell (PZ1);
2. where two or more land on one point, the first in draw's drawing order keeps it -- `byDrawingOrder`, `order` and then id (`model/stacking.mjs:11-14`) -- and each other, in that order, moves to the nearest free point of the new lattice to where it stood: free meaning no anchor keeps it and no earlier displaced anchor took it; nearest by Euclidean distance from its old point, equal distances going to the greater `y`, then the greater `x`; found by walking square rings about the old point's nearest position until no further ring can hold a point as near, every point of ring `k` lying at least `k - 1/2` squares away (PZ3; o:F5);
3. each zone's two corners move to the nearest points of the new zone lattice, so its edges stay on the lines; a side whose two corners land on one line -- a zone narrower than the new square, which would close to nothing, refused by its own row (`plugins/zones/zone-kind.mjs:50-51`) -- becomes one square, about the device-lattice point nearest that side's old centre (PZ2; o:F3);
4. every size -- a device's span, frame, sockets and content in the unit of 60 -- is kept (Q3: "scalaing a grid has no impact on individual node sizes"; PZ18), and links, pipes and groups follow their anchors, their routes derived again as today;
5. the notice says how many anchors moved, and names each anchor moved to a free point, each zone kept one square wide or high by step 3, and each zone whose enclosed anchors -- those strictly inside it, judged on every anchor's final point, a displaced one's included -- changed.

`size-change-lib.mjs` is this transaction as one function, and `size-change.expected.json` its result for every pinned production document and stored board at 40, 80, 120 and 240 -- every anchor's point, every zone's rect, the displaced anchors, the zones kept a square and those whose anchors changed -- the oracle move 15 is checked against (o:F4).
