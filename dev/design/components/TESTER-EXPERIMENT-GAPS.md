# Gap log - input tester built from snap-grid, input-binding, input-capture, vector-scene (v0.1.0)

Each entry: component | where I was reading | what was missing or unclear | workaround | severity | cost.
Severity: blocked = the four components cannot do it (worked around outside them); costly = real time or code; minor.

G1. all four | CONTRACT.md "Reference", docs/DECISIONS.md D2, many rules (SG2, SG5, SG9, IB1, IB2 rule 5, I10/I13) | The contract of record is "section 3 of draw's design (apnex/drawv2)". Rules are explained by comparison with draw's files (`zones/zone-keys.mjs:28-33`, `app/src/painter.js:29-32`) and by "the design's D6/section 6.3". I was not allowed to read them. | Ignored them; the rules can be read on their own terms. | minor | about 10 min of reading prose that points at code I could not see.

G2. all four | CONTRACT.md as a whole | About 1,270 lines of dense, formal contract. A consumer needs maybe 10% of it: the Use section, the fact/outcome shapes, and a few behaviours. Most of the text is about argument-reading order, Proxy traps and -0. There is no consumer quick reference. | Read everything; picked out the shapes. | costly | the largest single time cost of the build.

G3. input-capture | IC4, Fit criteria "the wheel or a pinch as facts: not yet ... the gesture tester's scope" | No wheel facts. The contract names the tester as the consumer this is waiting for. | My own `wheel` listener builds a fact-shaped object (converted with `scene.toDrawing`) and asks the same binding table, so the claim decides `preventDefault`. | blocked (within the four) | 12 lines.

G4. input-capture | IC4, Fit criteria "the context menu suppressed: not a menu handler (D5)" | Right-click on the surface opens the browser menu unless the app adds its own listener. A tester that shows right-clicks needs this. | My own `contextmenu` listener; a `context-menu` row in the table decides the claim. | blocked (within the four) | 4 lines.

G5. input-capture IC4 + input-binding Fit criteria "held keys tracked: not yet" | Capture listens for no `blur` or `visibilitychange`, and binding tracks no held keys. A key held while the window loses focus never sends key-up, so it stays lit. Held-key state is the core of a keyboard tester, and neither component owns it. | The app keeps its own map of held keys (code to {repeats, modifier}) and has its own window `blur` listener that clears it and cancels a live gesture with `pointerLost`. | blocked (blur) / costly (held keys) | about 25 lines.

G6. input-binding | IB9 (gesture `button`), IB11 (pointerUp "otherwise ... a live gesture stays live"), Requests table | Pointer Events report a second button pressed or released during a press as a pointermove (IC4 says so). The last release can be a different button from the one that opened the gesture. A gesture declared with `button: 0`, left pressed, then right pressed, then left released, then right released: the final up does not commit, and the gesture stays live with no press. There is no request to commit, only `startGesture`, `becomeGesture` and `cancelGesture`, so the app cannot end it correctly. I checked this against the release in Node. | Declared every gesture with `button: null` ("committed by any release"), so a gesture ends on the release of the last button. The session knows nothing of chorded buttons, so the app compares `fact.buttons` itself and gives chorded moves their own rows. | costly | about 30 min to reason through; it would have shipped as a stuck-gesture bug.

G7. input-capture IC8 (`takePointer`) + vector-scene VS9 (`itemOf`), and vector-scene's Edges paragraph | Once the pointer is taken, the platform sends every pointer event to the surface. So `itemOf(target)` answers null for the whole of a captured drag and for its drop. vector-scene's Edges say the tester "asks `itemOf` with `input-capture`'s target to know which key a pointer is over". Neither contract says this stops working while the pointer is held. Also, an overlay drawn under the pointer in another layer (my drag line) becomes the target, and `itemOf` names that item instead. | Hit-testing by geometry with snap-grid: each key's block of cells goes into an owner map, then `grid.nearest(at)` and `regionContains(span(p,p).covering, at)` find the owner. The `hit` function uses `itemOf` only for items in the keys and mouse layers, and the fx and hud layers are declared with `pointer-events: none`. | costly | about 12 lines; about 30 min, partly lost to G8.

G8. test harness (no component ships one) | input-capture I10 / vector-scene I10 say they drive trusted input over CDP; README "Test" | The Chrome/CDP drivers are in each repository's `test/` folder, not in the package or the docs, so I wrote my own (cdp.mjs, 62 lines). A quirk cost time: a CDP `Input.dispatchMouseEvent` `mouseMoved` must name the held button in `button` (not just in `buttons`). Otherwise Chrome does not treat it as a drag, and pointer capture never takes effect: `hasPointerCapture` is true but there is no `gotpointercapture` and events are not retargeted. I proved this with a page that uses no components. | Name the held button on every move. | costly | about 20 min, and 62 lines of harness.

G9. input-binding | IB11 "Inputs" table, IB10 | A gesture starts on the press (`pressMatched` gives `start`), not when the threshold is crossed, so every click is a start then a commit. "Drag" is not a phase. The app watches `pointerMove(...).crossed` and asks `becomeGesture`. Trap: passing `from` to `becomeGesture` restarts the press (`beyond` goes back to false), and the drop then comes out as a click. | Became `drag-*` on `crossed`, without `from`. | minor | 4 lines; the contract does say it, in IB10.

G10. input-binding | IB11 "An application that resolves a press calls `pressMatched` once, and again only when it answered `cancel`" | Every consumer has to write the same down-match-pressMatched-cancel-pressMatched sequence, then route move/up/cancel facts to the session and outcomes to bodies. capture and binding are designed as a pair, but there is no adapter between them. | Wrote it (onDown/onMove/onUp and the deliver switch). | costly | about 40 lines of glue I would expect a pair this tightly designed to provide.

G11. input-binding | Fit criteria "a hold, or a double click decided by time: not yet"; input-capture "a double click is the platform's own event" | A double click shows as Left click, Left click, Double click. Nothing can say a click is the first half of a double, and there is no hold or long-press. | Accepted; I show all three. | minor | 0 lines.

G12. input-binding | Duty / IB1 (the table reads facts as opaque) | I used the table to name gesture outcomes as well as raw inputs: synthesized `{ type: "end", gesture, trigger }` facts match rows such as "Right drag". That way the legend (help listing), the log and the overlap check all come from one table. Nothing says whether this is intended use; it works because facts are opaque. | Did it. | minor | none, but a guess.

G13. input-capture | IC5 | Facts hold no time ("Not a clock"), not even `event.timeStamp` as data, so a tester cannot show hold duration or repeat rate without its own clock. There is no `location` and no `getModifierState`, so CapsLock and NumLock lock state cannot be shown. | Not built; I show repeat counts instead of durations. | minor | -

G14. vector-scene | VS1/VS4 ("drawing coordinates: the root's own, while no view is declared"; pan/zoom "not yet") | There is no view. Setting a `viewBox` on the root seems to be left to the app, but the contract only says what the scene writes, not what the app may write on the root. Layer attributes can only be set at `openScene` ("the application writes nothing on it"). | Set `viewBox` on the root before opening; declared `pointer-events` on the layers at open. | minor | a guess.

G15. vector-scene VS10 + snap-grid SG6 | `inView` needs `view.scale` (screen units per plane unit). The scene converts points but does not answer a scale, and gives no resize signal. | Scale from two `toScreen` calls; my own `resize` listener redraws the dots. | minor | 4 lines.

G16. snap-grid | SG8/SG9 | Building keys from the grid works well: key unit = 4 cells of pitch 10, origin at pitch/2, so `span(from,to).covering` lands on exact edges. There is no inverse question, "which of my blocks owns this point". `enclosing` answers the smallest block for a rect, not ownership. Pitch must be an integer (fine at quarter units). | Owner map in the app (see G7). | minor | 6 lines.

G17. none of the four | - | No key layout data (positions, widths, labels for KeyboardEvent.code), and no locale mapping: an AZERTY user sees QWERTY labels. | Wrote an 87-key ANSI tenkeyless layout by hand from the UI Events code spec. | costly | 61 lines.
